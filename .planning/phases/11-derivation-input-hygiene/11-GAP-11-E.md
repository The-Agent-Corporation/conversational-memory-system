### Answers to the three open questions

**1. "Which rule is leaking? … Determine this before proposing a change."**
None of them alone — **three independent causes across 11 offenders**, established by Plan 11-22's
evidence (`11-GAP-11-E-FINDING.md`) and independently verified by the orchestrator:

- **H-0 (5 offenders)** — not a leak at all. `derivationContents` returned a single-entry tool segment
  unmodified (`derivation-hygiene.ts:216`, `segment.entries.length === 1`), which was **ROADMAP SC1's
  locked decision** working exactly as specified.
- **H-i (2 offenders)** — every `SYNTHETIC_RULES` entry was an anchored `startsWith`, so a marker
  embedded mid-event was invisible to `classifyEvent`. A rebuild alone reproduces this unchanged.
- **H-iii (4 offenders)** — the live `daemon_contraction_checkpoint` was pinned at `hygiene-rules/v1`;
  Plan 11-20's v2 rules had never been applied to the corpus at all.

The original framing ("which rule is leaking") contained a false premise: the residual was not one
misbehaving rule but a specification decision, a matcher limitation, and an unapplied rule version.

**2. "Is a rebuild sufficient?"**
**No — necessary but not sufficient, and not safe until a separate defect was fixed.** A rebuild alone
fixes only H-iii's 4 offenders; H-0 required reversing SC1 and H-i required changing the matcher, both
code/spec changes that a rebuild cannot substitute for. Because `PROCESSOR_VERSION` embeds
`HYGIENE_RULE_VERSION`, all three fixes shipped as `hygiene-rules/v3` and one rebuild applied them
together. The rebuild also turned out to be actively unsafe before Plan 11-27: 11-25's rehearsal
measured it resurrecting **1,330** of the 26,643 anchors Plan 11-18 had permanently deleted, every one
carrying raw `[Tool call:` text (GAP-11-G). Post-fix the measured resurrection is **0**. The concern
recorded in this question — "if the rules themselves leak, a rebuild reproduces the leak and is wasted
work on a 4.6 GB corpus" — was correct in spirit and landed on a different mechanism than anticipated.

**3. "Are the four queries hitting the same root cause or several?"**
**Several**, as above. Inspecting individually rather than generalizing was the right call and is what
produced the three-cause split. Concretely at `final-v2`: Q1/Q3/Q4/Q10's tool-call text is gone
(H-0/H-iii); Q12's two `task-notification` summaries are gone (H-iii); Q2's two remain and are the
**H-i class**, confirmed by direct inspection to be `[SILENT]` mentioned 4 and 2 times respectively,
**never at a line start**, always backtick-quoted inside a markdown table cell or design-prose bullet.
The product correctly classifies both `conversational`; only the probe's deliberately over-matching bare
`includes` scan flags them. Making these "pass" would require the unanchored matching this phase
rejected on false-positive grounds. **They are the expected residue, not a defect, and no follow-on work
is proposed for them.**

### The two anomalies recorded alongside

**Anomaly 1 — "harness text went 1/12 → 0/12 → 1/12."** Dissolved rather than explained: Plan 11-19
showed the v1 harness detector was a hand-rolled 3-pattern subset of the 13 shipped `SYNTHETIC_RULES`,
so all three of those figures were measurement artifacts and are not comparable to anything. The
hypothesis recorded here — "an anchor masking a summary" — was never confirmed and is now moot; the v2
harness re-measured all arms from scratch.

**Anomaly 2 — "the probe's harness detector may under-count."** **Confirmed, and it did.** Q12's
visible `<task-notification>` scaffolding was invisible to both the probe (fixed in Plan 11-19, which
rewrote the probe to use the shipped `classifyEvent`/`SYNTHETIC_MARKERS`) and the product (fixed in Plan
11-20, which added the `task-notification` rule on a counted census of 1,060 events). At `final-v2` Q12
carries 0 flagged items under all three predicates.

No new gap is raised for it — it is the same 350-anchor decision already on Plan 11-14's list, now with
measured evidence that at least one of the 350 reaches rank 0 of a real query.

## What it is

GAP-11-D established that anchors were injecting raw `[Tool call: ...]` text and closed that path
(forward gate `4d17978`, live prune `fe9adcb`). Tool-call text in injection packets fell from 10/12
queries to **4/12**, and no query's top-ranked memory is a raw tool-call payload any more.

The four survivors — **Q1, Q3, Q4, Q10** — all have **non-empty `sourceSummaryIds`** (2, 3, 2, 3
respectively). Per the discriminator established in `11-RECALL-PROBE.md`'s diagnostic, only
`rankSummary` sets a non-empty `summaryIds`; `kind: "anchor"` and `kind: "box_context"` items produce
an empty list. So the winning memory in all four cases **is summary-derived**.

The derivation-hygiene rebuild (Plans 11-01/11-02) reduced tool-call text in summaries — the corpus
audit that motivated Phase 11 measured 38% of summaries containing verbatim `[Tool call:` argument
text, and the rebuild collapsed tool runs into activity descriptions — but did not eliminate it.

## Why this is not GAP-11-D reopened

GAP-11-D was an *unfiltered derivation path* — anchors were created with no hygiene gate at all and
stored `turn.content` verbatim. That path is now gated forward and pruned backward, with no anchors of
class `tool_activity` or `synthetic_injected` remaining in the live corpus.

This gap is different in kind: summaries **did** pass through the hygiene classifier, and tool-call
text survived anyway. That points at the collapse rules or the summarizer's input selection, not at a
missing gate. The fix is therefore not "apply the same predicate somewhere new."

## Open questions a fix must answer first

1. **Which rule is leaking?** Is the residual text surviving `collapseText`, or entering via a
   summarizer input path that `derivationContents` does not filter, or is it in older summaries the
   rebuild did not regenerate? Determine this before proposing a change.
2. **Is a rebuild sufficient?** `rebuild-derivations` exists and is the natural remediation if the
   current rules would produce clean summaries from the same events. If the rules themselves leak, a
   rebuild reproduces the leak and is wasted work on a 4.6 GB corpus.
3. **Are the four queries hitting the same root cause or several?** Four queries is a small enough
   set to inspect individually rather than generalize from.

## Two anomalies to resolve alongside (they may change the numbers above)

1. **Harness text went 1/12 → 0/12 → 1/12.** Q2 regained harness text after the anchor prune. Leading
   hypothesis: the anchor previously winning Q2 was pruned, exposing a harness-text summary beneath —
   meaning the 0/12 was an anchor masking a summary, not a fix. Unconfirmed.
2. **The probe's harness detector may under-count.** Q12's `after-anchor-prune` preview is literally
   `<task-notification> <task-id>...` — visible harness scaffolding — yet `containsHarnessText`
   reports false. If the detector does not recognise `<task-notification>`, the harness column is
   optimistic in **all three runs**. Check the detector before drawing conclusions from the harness
   column.
